# Bounded campaign fault episodes

The protected original campaign uses a 240-second whole-campaign budget and reserves 45 seconds before waiting for each child; its first child was therefore stopped near 195 seconds. The captured first-child profile recorded startup 17.8 seconds, restarted socket connection 60.64 seconds, and 56.83 seconds awaiting faulted fighter input acknowledgements by frame 112. Polling-condition work was a small fraction of those waits. The driver already uses NullRHI. No renderer, seed, frame, navigation or offline-playback coverage is removed and no cap is increased.

The replacement fault schedule retains a lobby episode and two combat episodes: frame 35 on the existing viewer and fighter routes, and frame 80 on the newly joining viewer and fighter routes, configured before authentication. Each route independently drops a two-datagram burst upstream and downstream, then holds one datagram and delivers its successor twice before releasing the held datagram at fixed 20/23/26 ms offsets. Packet records include episode, direction, configured drop count and actual drop index. The schedule becomes healthy automatically after its finite witnesses; all witnesses must be delivered by frame 60 or 100 respectively before reset. Exact input acknowledgements and both native state comparisons continue across the healthy intervals and after the repeated episode. The original five unauthorized-control iterations and healthy organizer operations remain.

The two opposite-seat inputs are submitted together using fresh nonces. Both fixture receipts and correlated accepted room deliveries are required before the authoritative step. All three 160-frame histories, both battle seeds, delays 0/1/600, navigation operations, exports and independent offline comparisons remain. A duplex UDP unit test proves both burst losses, reordered/duplicated arrivals, labeled records and healthy traffic in both directions. The existing default single-direction finite schedule remains compatible with lifecycle tests.

Python compilation and all 11 host-only tests pass. Full runtime cost must be measured with the protected engine before any overall budget change is considered.
