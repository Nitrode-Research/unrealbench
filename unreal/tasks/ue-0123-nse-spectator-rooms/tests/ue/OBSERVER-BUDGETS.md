# Scenario and external image observation budgets

UI PNG and render BMP evaluators publish their complete immutable request batches before one global wait. That wait is now 90 seconds, with a CLI range of 0 through 90 seconds. It is not a per-image allowance. Images, requests, schemas, field definitions, first observations, hashes, equality checks and missing-evidence failures are unchanged.

Neutral reader qualification reported UI batches finishing only 35 of 36 and 34 of 36 items by roughly 45.7 seconds, with no invalid, unreadable or conflicting first observations. An independent agent reported model-capacity pressure. A nine-item BMP batch completed in 39.012 seconds. This supports a larger finite external-reader allowance; it does not establish that every future reader batch will finish in 90 seconds.

| Watchdog | Previous seconds | New seconds | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Standalone UI review | 70 | 115 | 90-second observation plus the existing 25-second process overhead |
| Standalone rendered DVR review | 70 | 115 | Same observation and process overhead |
| Standalone retained-stage review | 70 | 115 | Same observation and process overhead |
| SixProcessAuthority driver | 1200 | 1245 | One embedded observer wait grows by 45 seconds |
| EndpointWidgets driver | 240 | 285 | One embedded observer wait grows by 45 seconds |
| LifecycleAndSeatTransfer driver | 1500 | 1545 | One embedded observer wait grows by 45 seconds |
| CombinedLifecycleFuzz driver | 270 | 720 | The 600-second action driver, 90-second observation and 30-second overhead |
| FuzzMetamorphicCampaign driver | 285 | 525 | A 480-second campaign allocation plus the existing 45-second outer margin |

Candidate command/reply waits remain 30 seconds. Startup and ordinary readiness waits retain their existing explicit limits, including 10, 30, 60 and 120 seconds. Native start, frame-pump and presentation deadlines remain unchanged. The action/frame schedules are fixed and finite. These individual deadlines remain the candidate completion checks; the composite driver watchdogs now leave more room for external interpretation of captured evidence. No candidate action is retried, selection recaptured or gameplay tick added because of reader latency.

The combined action driver receives `--driver-deadline 600` with a 720-second outer cap. V3 reached the former 240-second action deadline during generation 1 after both required recovery paths passed. The first generation reached frame 60 at 71.328 seconds, recovery one completed at 136.438 seconds, recovery two at 200.219 seconds, and generation 1 selection at 207.797 seconds. All three generations remain required. The new action allocation allows 120 seconds per generation, two normal 60-second retirement windows and a further 120-second margin. These are planning allocations, not new individual phase guarantees. The outer cap adds the unchanged 90-second observer allowance and 30 seconds of overhead. No action, assertion, fault, input or generated checkpoint is removed. FuzzMetamorphicCampaign receives a 480-second campaign budget and 525-second wrapper. Its children use `--no-recovery`, so they do not enter the recovery-only network image review. GGPO, crash, fresh offline replay and cleanup watchdogs are unchanged.

The Fuzz increase is a calibrated forecast, not an observed Fuzz timeout. V3 completed the first case's DVR, GGPO and decoded-payload audit at 94.282 seconds with 3842 assertions, then failed its final export assertion. It did not finish a passing case or child. The fixed campaign requires three cases across two child process groups, semantic comparisons and complete fresh offline replay. Three repetitions of the measured audit duration total 282.846 seconds before remaining exports and offline work. Shared startup may save time, but the former 195-second shared child allowance was too tight for this forecast. The new 480-second allocation leaves 435 seconds shared by both children after the unchanged 45-second reserve, before elapsed orchestration. That is 152.154 seconds above the uncredited 282.846-second projection for unmeasured export tails, cleanup and timing variation. The 45-second reserve remains available for comparison and offline replay; it is not a new per-operation limit. Python does not check the campaign deadline during offline replay, so the 525-second outer watchdog remains the final whole-campaign bound. The existing 1500-second per-child ceiling and its minimum with remaining campaign time are unchanged. Every case, input, action, assertion and replay frame remains required. This forecast does not guarantee completion within the new limit.

The three standalone observer increases add 135 seconds. Network, endpoint and lifecycle add another 135 seconds; the original combined observer correction added 90 seconds. That observer successor totaled 7065 seconds from a 6705-second baseline. The measured V3 Combined action-budget correction adds 360 seconds, bringing the nominal allocation to 7425 seconds. The Fuzz forecast correction adds 240 seconds, for a nominal allocation of 7665 seconds. The separately controlled 8100-second aggregate ceiling is unchanged and leaves 435 seconds for remaining overhead. This arithmetic is a finite allocation, not a promise about runtime cost or reader availability. A missing observation still fails at the deadline, and a valid negative first observation still fails the relevant visual predicate.

Timing checks use synthetic PNG/BMP bytes, a fake monotonic clock and scripted first observations. They verify that a response at 50 seconds keeps its actual value and that missing evidence fails at 90 seconds. They do not provide Unreal or live image-reader qualification.
